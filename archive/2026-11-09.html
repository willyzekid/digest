Last 7 days (a few late-October items are included where a deadline or rollout falls in this window)

Air Transat opens Staria, its first loyalty program since suspending BonBon

Air Transat opened enrolment in Staria on 2 November 2026 after a closed test phase that began in August. Members earn points on purchases, collect stars by completing missions, and can pool points with family and friends in a constellation, while Porter Airlines flights earn at the same rates as Air Transat flights. Desjardins and Visa are named strategic partners for co-branded credit cards, with card details to follow when the cards launch in 2027.

  • Pooled points and mission-based stars point to a shift from pure spend-based earn toward engagement and household-level mechanics.
  • Letting a rival airline earn at the same rate shows coalitions forming even between direct competitors.
  • A bank co-brand means card economics will likely fund much of the program.

Recommendation: Benchmark household pooling and mission mechanics against your own program, and test whether a limited partner-earn arrangement could extend reach without new capital.

Dubai launches Access Club, a city-run loyalty membership with a five-year visa

Dubai launched the invitation-only Dubai Access Club, run by GDRFA Dubai and the Department of Economy and Tourism, with reports dating the launch to 1 November 2026. Eligible repeat visitors can obtain a five-year multiple-entry tourist visa, and reported perks include hotel upgrades, lounge access and shopping privileges. Emirates, Accor and Visa are launch partners, and invitations are being extended through select loyalty programmes and multinational companies linked to those partners.

  • A benefit controlled by a government, the visa, is something no brand program can replicate, which moves loyalty toward destination-level ownership of the customer.
  • Partner programs become the acquisition channel, so partners' member bases are effectively the funnel.
  • One report puts the application fee at AED 4,032.50 including visa fees, which makes this a paid tier rather than a free scheme.

Recommendation: Explore whether your premium tier could be offered as a distribution partner to destination or government-linked access programs.

Amazon lets shoppers text Alexa for Shopping to place orders

Amazon launched text-message ordering for Alexa for Shopping on 6 November 2026, initially by invitation to a subset of US customers with wider access promised in the coming weeks. The agent can find products, build carts, complete purchases and send delivery updates over text, and conversations carry across text, the Amazon Shopping app, Amazon.com and Alexa-enabled devices.   • Loyalty identity and offers must be recognisable when the interaction starts in a text thread.

Recommendation: Audit whether your loyalty identity and offers can be recognised and honoured in a messaging-based, agent-led purchase flow.

EU Council fixes its Digital Omnibus position on cookies and consent

EU ambassadors agreed the Council's negotiating mandate on the Digital Omnibus on 21 October 2026, and talks with the European Parliament can begin only once Parliament adopts its own position. The Council text returns cookie rules to Article 5(3) of the ePrivacy Directive with six consent exemptions, including contextual advertising measurement, reversing the Commission's proposal to move them into the GDPR as Article 88a. Consent remains the default requirement for cookies and similar technologies, and Parliament's draft report from 22 June had not been put to a committee vote as of mid-September.

  • Consented first-party data stays the foundation of personalization in Europe.
  • Measurement exemptions could ease analytics, but the scope is still being negotiated.
  • Timelines remain uncertain until Parliament sets its position.

Recommendation: Keep investing in consented first-party data and loyalty-linked identity, and treat any exemptions as upside rather than the plan.

Emerging themes

Agents are becoming the customer interface

Amazon lets shoppers text Alexa for Shopping to place orders, and Walmart introduces agent profiles, spending limits and an Agent Gateway. Both retailers are building ways for an agent to act on a customer's behalf while keeping the transaction inside their own account system. Cordial launches a headless CDP that marketers can query from Claude and ChatGPT, which shows the same pattern on the marketing side. Loyalty identity, consent and offers will have to work when the buyer is an agent, and the retailers moving first are setting the terms.

Partnership realignment and tier compression

Hyatt and American Airlines wind down their partnership as Hyatt pairs with Delta, and Best Western Rewards will merge into WorldHotels Rewards on 5 January 2027 with fewer tiers. Marriott ends sales and renewals of the paid mycitizenM+ membership, and Southwest announces a 2027 Rapid Rewards overhaul that decouples card bonuses from Companion Pass. Each move simplifies structure while removing a benefit some members valued. The moments of change, such as linking deadlines and tier notifications, are when experience quality and trust are decided.

Coalitions widen through new kinds of partners

Air Transat opens Staria, its first loyalty program since suspending BonBon, with Porter earning at the same rates and bank partners for cards. Tesco opens Clubcard points at more than 500 online partner brands, and Dubai launches Access Club, a city-run loyalty membership with a five-year visa, with Emirates, Accor and Visa as launch partners. Partners now include rivals, affiliate networks and governments rather than only same-sector brands. This widens reach quickly but shifts economics toward partner funding and shared data. Program owners need clear rules on who owns the customer relationship once the coalition grows.

Reflection questions

  1. If your best customers delegate purchases to an agent, which parts of your loyalty proposition survive, and which have to be redesigned?
  2. Where in your program are you relying on a single partner or a card issuer for economics, and what would you do if the terms changed within a year?
  3. How would you decide whether a status or tier change improves long-term value, given that members only notice what they lose?

Emerging themes

Agents move from demos to governed deployment

Walmart sets guardrails for third-party AI shopping agents and Shopify turns on agentic checkout by default for eligible merchants, while Anthropic cuts its internal agent evaluations off from the live internet after agent incidents. Commercial platforms are opening their doors to agents at the same time as labs are tightening containment. The common thread is control: spending limits, default approvals, opt-outs and network isolation. Organisations that define their agent policies early will be better placed than those that react to incidents.

Cheaper and more open models change the economics

OpenAI releases GPT-6.1 Terra at half the API price of its predecessor, Mistral publishes open weights for Mistral Large 4, and model trackers list Meta's Muse Spark Open as a large open-weight release. Falling prices and open licenses make more use cases viable and give regulated firms self-hosting options. At the same time, financing for AI compute reaches new scale amid bubble warnings, which means the cost trend rests on capital that may not be permanent. Buyers should negotiate flexibility rather than assume prices keep falling.

Workforce effects are real but hard to measure

Microsoft opens a voluntary exit offer to about 60,000 employees, while Challenger data shows AI-cited US job cuts falling in October to the lowest monthly level of the year. Companies are reshaping headcount to fund AI spending, yet the share of cuts officially attributed to AI swings widely. Floqer's open-source agents for B2B revenue teams show where junior research roles are being redefined rather than simply removed. Leaders need their own measures of role change instead of relying on headline attribution.

Reflection questions

  1. Which decisions in your business would you be comfortable letting an agent make within a spending or risk limit, and who sets that limit?
  2. If model prices keep falling and open-weight options improve, where does your competitive advantage sit, and how would you protect it?
  3. How will you redesign entry-level roles so that people still build judgement when agents do the first draft of the work?

Inspiring story

Emerging themes

Success fees and per-use pricing replace seats and retainers

Freight Clawback, Margin Lab, Bill Whisperer and Archive Royalty all charge only when a measured outcome occurs, whether recovered dollars, extra margin, verified savings or answers delivered. AI makes each outcome measurable and cheap to pursue, so the seller can carry the risk. This turns buyers who would never approve a software budget into customers. The pattern favours founders who can verify results credibly.

Agents become customers, and a market forms around them

Agent Allowance, Agent Rate Card, Mistake Cover and Preference Vault all assume that software acts for people and needs identity, limits, pricing and insurance. Each sits on a different side of the transaction, with the household, the retailer, the insurer and the data owner as the buyers. The infrastructure layer is still open, and incumbents are building their own controls first. Neutral, cross-platform tools could be valuable if standards emerge.

Behavior shifts create buyers that incumbents ignore

Half-Portion, Kin Ledger, Flex Wallet and Payout Float each target a group whose behavior is changing, namely GLP-1 diners, family caregivers, households with batteries and workers with irregular income. These customers do not fit existing product categories, and they are reachable with lightweight software. Spending pressure and ageing populations make the demand durable. Small teams can serve these gaps before large companies reorganise around them.
